emotional process geometry

Emotions aren't quite what many of us think they are. What we're usually concerned with isn't what they are so much as how they behave. Emotions don't behave like things. They aren't discrete categories, and they don't move in simple binaries like feeling/unfeeling, good/bad, like/dislike, or happy/sad. Instead, they seem to exist on a continuum that is always in motion and always changing.

Listen to how we talk about them: emotions rise and settle, spread or stick, loop or pass. They can be held inside, or expressed and released. This is the language of motion.

One way to picture this continuum is as a river, constantly flowing and changing. Like a river, our feelings shift from moment to moment. At times there's turbulence; at other times, a peaceful, steady stream. Some days the river is large and powerful, and other days it's just a trickle. Seeing emotion this way brings dynamics and forces into view, and from them, structure and even patterned movement begin to emerge. My thinking has been that if we can describe these dynamics and forces accurately, we can build a model of that movement that's intuitive and visual enough to connect the unseen dots.

Showing this motion has been the focus of my research and much of my creative work for the past couple of years, and I'm finally at a place where I want to share it.

Eight years of social work, much of it in an anxiety program, alongside my audiovisual performance practice, pushed me to understand, map, and begin building a framework. But things only started to click when I brought in my spiritual and contemplative practice and my own experience. Much of this took shape during one of the most difficult periods of my life. I wanted to understand why I felt the things I felt, and I believed that if I could see the map, I could find my way out. But one of the biggest lessons was that understanding something in my head could never replace the knowing that comes from experiencing it with the heart.

The process has given me tools and a deeper awareness for understanding my own pain and healing, though I'm still just beginning to integrate these ideas into my own life. It's very much a work in progress and a living record of my own journey into the self. Still, I've come to believe that if we start to see all the places this continuum of emotion touches, and connect pieces that seem unrelated, a coherent structure comes into view, one that is beautiful and consistent.

Any model like this is only a map. It isn't what emotion actually "looks like," and like any attempt to capture a dynamic process, it has real limits. But an approach that leans on images and movement as much as words, and that is integrative and systematic, may be able to reach parts of emotional experience that more traditional ways of thinking miss. That's something I hope to show over time.

In the posts ahead, I'll share pieces of the system and how it works, along with my attempts to represent these dynamics visually. My sincere hope is that this helps people understand their own emotions a little better and explore how it relates to current theories of emotional regulation, healing strategies, and therapeutic practice.
