emotional process geometry

we tend to treat emotions as things. but when we want to understand them better, what we're usually concerned with has less to do with what they are and more to do with how they behave. and they don't behave like things. they aren't discrete objects with a clear form, and they can't be reduced to the categories and labels we assign them. they rarely operate in simple binaries like feeling/unfeeling, good/bad, like/dislike, or happy/sad. instead, they seem to resist fixed definition, as if they exist on a continuum that is always in motion and always changing.

consider how we talk about them: emotions rise and settle, spread or stick, loop or pass. they can be held inside, or expressed and released. this is the language of motion, dynamics, and change. it describes something that moves less like a solid object and more like a fluid.

one way to picture this continuum is as a river, constantly flowing and changing. like a river, our feelings shift from moment to moment. at times there's turbulence; at other times, a peaceful, steady stream. some days the river is large and powerful, and other days it's just a trickle. seeing emotion this way brings dynamics and forces into view, and from them, structure and even patterned movement begin to emerge. my thinking with this project has been that if we could describe these dynamics and forces accurately, maybe we could build a model of that movement that's both intuitive and visual enough to reveal the unseen connections between the many pieces involved in how a feeling moves.

showing this motion has been the focus of my research and much of my creative work for the past couple of years, and i'm finally at a place where i want to share it.

eight years of social work, much of it in an anxiety program, alongside my audiovisual performance practice, gave me the initial push and the tools to try to understand, map, and begin building a framework. things only really started to click, though, when i brought my spiritual and contemplative practice and my own personal experience into the work. much of this took shape during one of the most difficult periods of my life. i wanted to understand why i felt the things i felt, and on some level, i believed that if i could see the map, i could find my way out of the feelings. but one of the biggest lessons in all of this was realizing that this was a kind of false escape. understanding something in my head could never replace the knowing that comes from experiencing a feeling with the heart. the process as a whole has given me new tools and a deeper awareness for understanding my own pain and healing, as well as my happiness and joy, but i'm still just beginning to integrate these ideas into my own life. it's all very much a work in progress, and it exists as a living record of my own journey into the self. still, i've come to believe that if we start to see all the places this continuum of emotion touches, and try to connect the pieces that seem unrelated, a coherent structure comes into view, one that, like the river, is beautiful, changing in its movement but consistent in its patterns.

any model like this is only a map. it isn't what emotion actually "looks like," and like any attempt to capture a dynamic process, it has real limits. but an approach that leans on images and movement as much as words, and that is integrative and systematic, may be able to reach parts of emotional experience that more traditional ways of thinking miss. that's something i hope to show over time.

in the posts ahead, i'll share pieces of the system and how it works, along with my attempts to represent these dynamics visually. my sincere hope is that this helps people understand their own emotions a little better and explore how it relates to current theories of emotional regulation, healing strategies, and therapeutic practice.

about the visual

the movement isn't animated. descriptions of how feelings move, including my own, were translated into mathematical forces, and the visual is generated live from them. because it includes small random fluctuations, it doesn't repeat.

a map of how a feeling moves, not a picture of what it is.
